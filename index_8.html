#510. New WhatsApp head wants to make the app easier to use, give its AI more chops

WhatsApp has grown into one of the most popular messaging apps in the world, used by over 3 billion people. But with that growth, the app has also become complex, packing in features like communities, channels, stories and business features, and a lot of people find it hard to navigate.

Kunal Shah, who took over leadership of the app’s development from Will Cathcart earlier this year, now wants to make the app and actions within it simpler to use. In a new note shared on Tuesday, Shah said the company wants to make voice notes, groups, stickers, and video “simpler and more expressive.”

WhatsApp has long also tried to develop an ecosystem that lets users explore products and make purchases, and Shah’s note indicates more of that is coming, hinting at partnerships with payment companies and telecom providers to enable bill payment, loans, and eSIM purchases.

Shah also said the company wants to use AI to surface important information to users.

“It should quickly find what you need, get things done, and make sure you never miss a booking or an appointment. The best technology saves you time, so you can get back to the connections that matter,” he wrote.

WhatsApp currently offers an AI assistant that can summarize messages and create drafts. It’s not clear if the company will use its Muse agent to collate information and send reminders to people.

I think of WhatsApp like a shared language, spoken by 3bn+ people. Sometimes we use words, and sometimes it’s a voice note to a parent, a sticker that ends an argument, or a “home safely” at 2am. Language is at the heart of everything we as people do. We write letters, tell stories, send hard messages and make plans for the future. A WhatsApp chat is often where we start.

Like any language, the people who use it shape it. They don’t wait for permission. They find new things to say, and new ways to say them. Often, our users show us what should come next based on how they’re using the app and communicating in the world.

I’ve spent the last few months listening and understanding what our users need and want from us and, and what I heard is simple: Help people stay close to those who matter to them, and get more done on WhatsApp.

First, we want to make connections richer.  Sometimes hearing someone’s voice, sending a joke, agreeing on a plan or knowing they’re safe is the whole point. People send over 10 billion voice messages and over 4 billion stickers on WhatsApp every day. As the way people communicate evolves, we will evolve these experiences with them. We’ll make voice notes, groups, stickers and video simpler and more expressive, because how people feel close keeps changing.

Second, we want anything you do to be as simple as sending a message. Too many things start in a chat and end with many more steps somewhere else: paying a bill, borrowing money, staying connected when you travel. Each handoff to another task costs time. We’re working with financial services and telecom partners so that finishing something feels as easy as starting it.

Third, we want to make sure AI makes it all more efficient. It should quietly find what you need, get things done and make sure you never miss a booking or an appointment. The best technology saves you time, so you can get back to the connections that matter.

None of this matters without trust. Trust takes years to earn and a moment to lose, and I don’t take it lightly. Whatever we build, WhatsApp must remain simple, reliable and private. These principles we’ve had for years aren’t changing. Your personal messages and calls stay end-to-end encrypted. That isn’t changing, and neither is our quality bar.

What happens inside WhatsApp only matters for what it makes possible outside it: more time together, stronger communities, and fewer barriers between people and the lives they want to live.

#507. Reddit is turning user conversations into AI-powered product reviews

Shortly after announcing a plan to transition its site to a logged-in experience, Reddit is debuting a feature that could help make it more of a destination for people doing product research via the web. The company on Tuesday introduced “Reddit Reviews,” a feature that organizes conversations about products into dedicated reviews that include the highlights and key takeaways from users across its communities.

The company notes that the new experience builds on one of the ways people are already using Reddit: to learn from others what products are really like, and how they fit into their everyday lives. These first-hand accounts and recommendations can help consumers determine whether or not a particular product is right for them.

However, given the rise of generative AI and bots, it’s an area that’s also ripe for “astroturfing” — a term that refers to when a brand fakes public support. With Reddit now effectively turning what was casual online research into more of a structured product, it could become a larger target for these types of fake reviews and recommendations than before.

To address this, Reddit’s announcement points to its rules and its latest measures to combat automated abuse and spam, as well as its practice involving community moderators, as tools it will use to keep the reviews on the site legit.

The Reddit Reviews feature itself taps into AI to organize the conversations people are having where they describe their product experiences. These posts will often see users discussing how a product works for them, or why it fails, or what the trade-offs are that someone should consider before buying.

The company has been testing the product reviews feature since February, initially with U.S. users in areas like Electronics, Video Games, and Health & Beauty products. In September, Reddit expanded the test to include the Home & Garden and Fashion & Apparel categories as well.

Now, the company says Reddit Reviews is rolling out to all U.S. users on iOS, Android, and web, with an initial selection focused on around 10,000 products. Over time, it will roll out to more categories and expand beyond the U.S.

To use the feature, Reddit users can search for a product or category in the app’s search bar or click on a highlighted product name they see in someone’s comment. From there, the user is taken to a screen where important conversations are summarized under a “what people are saying” heading. This will also include a visual breakdown that shows how many of those comments are positive, negative, or mixed, and what sort of things people said they liked or disliked about the item.

For instance, a user considering buying an espresso machine might want to know about things like how much counter space it takes up or whether it’s easier for beginners to understand how to use it, says Reddit. Users can then browse the comments, filter them by topic, or click to be taken to the original conversation, where they can ask more questions or get advice.

Users can also move from one product review back to the full category to weigh other, similar products that have been discussed to compare their options, the company said.

The feature arrives just ahead of the busy holiday shopping season, when people spend a lot more time doing online product research ahead of their bigger purchases.

The chip launch comes as hyperscalers like Microsoft, Amazon, and even AI labs like OpenAI and Anthropic are working on their own lines of AI-specific semiconductors and accompanying software in order to reduce their reliance on the dominance of Nvidia.

#491. Apple TV makes sign language interpretation a built-in accessibility feature

Apple TV is making its content more accessible to Deaf audiences with the launch of a new Sign Language Interpretation feature, the company announced on Tuesday. The feature will initially be available across a limited set of titles, starting this Friday with the release of “Being Heumann,” a biopic about disability rights activist Judy Heumann.

American Sign Language (ASL) and British Sign Language (BSL) will also be made available for Apple’s hit movie “F1.” Later this year, other titles will follow, including “Way of the Warrior Kid” and “Greyhound.”

What makes Apple’s offering different from other platforms is that the sign language version of the film is not a separate product — that is, it’s not a separate film designed just for Deaf viewers. Instead, Apple’s support for sign language is just a setting you enable that’s integrated with the main title, similar to how you could turn on or off closed captions.

When a title with the sign language option is playing, an interpreter appears in the bottom right of the screen, translating the spoken words into signs. Apple says it has partnered with a group of DASLs (Directors of Artistic Sign Language) to ensure that the interpreter for each film fits the tone of the film. Notably, the company is not using any AI-generated interpreters, as Apple says that’s not what the Deaf community has said it needs.

The feature is shipping in the latest release of iOS 27.2, and can be turned on through a new sign language option in Settings. After being enabled, a new button will appear in the supported Apple Original titles on Apple TV indicating that sign language is available.

These titles are labeled with a sign language icon that appears on the title page alongside other features like closed captions or audio descriptions. There’s also a new section under languages that will list the specific sign languages that are available with the given title.

To make the titles more discoverable, Apple TV will highlight which titles include the sign language option in a “Movies & Shows with American Sign Language” editorial collection. Meanwhile, users can easily surface supported titles via search by typing in keywords like “with ASL,” for example.

The new feature builds on Apple’s other investments in accessibility features, which it builds itself.

“At Apple, we believe everyone should be able to enjoy great stories and entertainment,” said Sarah Herrlinger, Apple’s senior director of Global Accessibility Policy and Initiatives, in a statement about the launch. “Bringing sign language interpretation to Apple TV is an exciting step forward in making the viewing experience more accessible and inclusive for Deaf audiences. Accessibility is a core value at Apple, and we’re proud to continue creating experiences that allow more people to enjoy the content they love,” she added.

#487. Sundrop turns your endless baby photos into recaps, time lapses, and more — without AI

New parents take a lot of pictures of their children, but those photos often end up languishing in photo galleries, never to be seen again after the initial snap and share.

A new app, Sundrop, aims to make those pictures more useful by turning them into weekly recaps, highlights, time lapses of the child growing up, and other cute scenes, like putting the child’s photo on a magazine cover or a rookie card.

While Google Photos and Apple Photos already provide extensive tools to organize and manage photos, Sundrop is taking aim at a particular niche: parents with a new baby (usually their first!) who take an overwhelming number of photos and want to do more with those pictures.

The app was developed over the past few months by the same team behind the read-it-later app, Matter: Ben Springwater, Sky Lan, and Rob Mackenzie (all dads themselves).

Notably, Sundrop claims it is designed to be private, and processes all photos locally on the device it is installed on. To make this possible, Sundrop taps into Apple’s Vision framework and on-device Core ML (machine learning) models instead of uploading photos to a cloud server or some other hosted AI service.

Both of the child’s parents can use Sundrop together by linking their accounts, which allows the app to sync their shared photos via Apple’s iCloud service. This feature, the company says, means the pictures aren’t sent to any external servers it runs.

In addition, the team notes that no generative AI is used in the creation of its scenes or highlights.

This differentiates it from many photo-editing apps on the market, including Google’s, which are increasingly tapping generative AI to create new versions of your photos.

To find highlights and recaps, Sundrop scans pictures in the parents’ respective Camera Rolls on their iPhones, skipping things like screenshots or duplicates. It then creates highlights or fun scenes, which it calls “drops.”

One of these is a recap of the week every Sunday, announced as a Lock Screen notification. Others can put two siblings side-by-side at the same age, generate a time-lapse series of photos or a video of a kid growing up, or cut out a photo of the child and place it into a layered template.

Parents can also manually edit the photos Sundrop uses to make its creations, or use the app to automatically organize the best photos into galleries with other similar shots.

The app costs $39.99 per year, and offers a week-long free trial. The annual plan also includes Family Sharing, so only one parent needs to purchase it.

#484. Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang’s affair with alleged Chinese spy: a timeline

Nvidia co-founder CEO Jensen Huang has never been as flashy or controversy-prone as AI leaders like OpenAI’s Sam Altman or SpaceXAI’s Elon Musk. Now, the 63-year-old industry kingpin has admitted that he cheated on his wife of 40 years in an affair that could have ramifications beyond Huang’s personal life and potentially impact national security.

United States officials believe that Huang’s likely romantic interest, Megaspeed executive Alice Huang, is a Chinese spy who has been feeding information back to Beijing. (Alice Huang, a Chinese citizen, is of no relation to Jensen Huang.)

Megaspeed, a Singapore-based AI infrastructure company, had reportedly purchased $4.8 billion in Nvidia chips over the last two years. The U.S. and Singapore have both previously investigated Megaspeed over suspicions that the firm was illegally sharing chips with China.

The U.S. and China are embroiled in a fierce technological arms race to build the most advanced AI infrastructure, which could shape each country’s economic future, military prowess, and capacity for innovation. Nvidia is valuable in this competition as one of the world’s largest makers of AI chips, which are essential for accelerating AI development. But as an American company, Nvidia faces government restrictions on the sale of its most powerful chips to Chinese entities.

As the controversy unfolds, we are organizing the most relevant events into a reverse-chronological timeline to keep you up to date.

The Nvidia CEO pulled out of upcoming speaking engagements at the ElevenLabs Summit and Anthropic CEO Forum.

Alice Huang and her family reportedly built a network of companies in China and Singapore to acquire Nvidia chips, including founding a Chinese company called Shanghai Century Litong. Founded in 2023, it became an Nvidia-preferred cloud partner, giving it priority access to chips, The Information reported. The Information also reported that Shanghai Century Litong facilitated buying $7.5 billion worth of Nvidia’s chips, including banned chips.

Nvidia’s head lawyer, Tim Teter, intervened in a 2025 U.S. government probe surrounding the suspected smuggling of AI chips by Megaspeed International, Bloomberg reported. Teter contacted Commerce Under Secretary Jeffrey Kessler asking questions about whether the Trump administration played a role in detaining Alice Huang.

In early 2025, Alice Huang and a business partner sought China-based investors for an Asian data center, promising they had no problem in securing GPUs, the Financial Times reported.

On Sunday night, President Trump weighed in on Huang’s controversy and showed support for the Nvidia CEO.

“I have not spoken to him about it. I just know he’s a fine person, but you never know what’s going on,” Trump said.

When asked if Huang should remain chief of Nvidia, Trump said, “I think so. Oh yeah, sure.”

Jensen Huang wrote in a letter to Nvidia employees — which was also published as a press release — that he cheated on his wife.

“I owe my family and all of you an apology,” Huang told his company. “I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did. I am deeply sorry.”

Huang did not mention Alice Huang by name in the message, but linked his infidelity to “the national security concerns raised in news reports.”

“Nvidia’s Board of Directors has initiated an independent investigation. I support the investigation and will cooperate fully. I love our country and our company. I am committed to protecting Nvidia and supporting America’s national security. I am proud and grateful to be an American,” Huang wrote.

Nvidia shares slid over 5% the following day.

Three Republican congressmen — Chairman John Moolenaar of the House Select Committee on China, Chairman Brian Mast of the House Foreign Affairs Committee, and Chairman Rick Crawford of the House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence — sent a request to Huang for a closed-door joint briefing with their three committees.

“Recent reports raise significant national security concerns regarding the [People’s Republic of China]’s access to sensitive U.S. technology and information,” the chairmen wrote. “The issue of PRC infiltration and access to cutting-edge U.S. technology is core to the investigative work of our three committees this Congress. It is vital that Congress determines the extent to which U.S. national security has been placed at risk.”

U.S. officials had been investigating Alice Huang and Megaspeed for more than a year when the New York Times broke the story in late October that the two executives also had a close relationship.

An Nvidia spokesperson denied that this relationship was something out of the ordinary.

“Jensen’s interactions with Ms. Huang are not unusual or unexpected. Meetings with customers are part of a CEO’s job; Jensen talks with thousands of customer executives every year,” Nvidia spokesperson John Rizzo said. He added that “if the U.S. government believes that any person is a threat to American industry, it would let us know.”

U.S. officials circulated a secret memo among policymakers that warned that Megaspeed’s Alice Huang, who had a relationship with Jensen Huang, was a spy for China’s Ministry of State Security. This memo was not public knowledge until it was later reported by the New York Times.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio supervised the drafting of the memo.

Around the same time, per the New York Times’ report, U.S. President Donald Trump was briefed about intelligence findings that possible Chinese spy Alice Huang had formed a close relationship with Jensen Huang. Trump did not act on this information.

The Nvidia CEO, an ally of Trump, has repeatedly advocated for the U.S. government to let Nvidia expand its business in China.

Megaspeed was confirmed to be under investigation by both the U.S. and Singaporean governments in October 2025. As a customer of Nvidia, Megaspeed was suspected of helping Chinese companies get around American embargoes on chip exports.

Megaspeed, a neocloud that used Nvidia chips, had been renting computing power to Chinese internet giant Alibaba and other Chinese companies, the Times reported. A Commerce Department official who had visited Megaspeed’s data center in Malaysia in 2024 had found a hoard of Nvidia equipment that was still packed in boxes and the concern was that this equipment was being directly shipped somewhere else, like China, rather than installed and sold as a cloud service.

Jensen Huang had been spotted socializing with Megaspeed executive Alice Huang as early as 2024 in Taiwan, where the Nvidia CEO was born.

These investigations into Megaspeed highlighted concerns about the degree to which Nvidia is able to track its chip exports. At the time, an Nvidia spokesperson told press that the company did not have “any reason to believe products had been diverted.”

“NVIDIA visited multiple Megaspeed sites yet again earlier this week and confirmed what we previously observed — Megaspeed is running a small commercial cloud, like many other companies throughout the world, as allowed by U.S. export control rules,” the Nvidia spokesperson said at the time.
