#561. JetBlue stock surges as company outlook calms investors

JetBlue Airways reported a bigger loss in the third quarter compared with last year, but shares surged as the carrier's upbeat forecast that it will return to profitability appeared to calm investors after a Monday rout.

JetBlue stock rose 18% in trading Tuesday after hitting a new low a day earlier as investors fretted about the company's financial future.

The Monday drop came after the Wall Street Journal reported Friday that a group of the airline's senior creditors is reportedly working with restructuring adviser PJT Partners.

"We have no intention, no plans to file for bankruptcy. We are not engaged with any restructuring company," CEO Joanna Geraghty told CNBC on Tuesday. "We are focused on delivering to our shareholders what we laid out for at the beginning of our turnaround plan."

She said there is a "fundamental disconnect between the narrative in the bond market and what our financial results actually demonstrate."

The New York airline late Monday reported that its revenue per available seat mile, a key metric measuring how much operating revenue a carrier generates for every seat flown one mile, would be up as much as 22% in the fourth quarter, bringing the annual increase to an expected 13.5% to 14.5%

"We're not seeing any meaningful pushback," Geraghty said in the interview.

JetBlue on Monday posted a net loss of $257 million for the three months ended Sept. 30, compared with a loss of $143 million a year earlier. The carrier's fuel bill rose 61% to $868 million.

The carrier said it ended the quarter with $2.2 billion in liquidity and that its unencumbered asset base is now $7 billion, including $2.8 billion in aircraft and engines.

JetBlue had total debt of $8.4 billion as of Sept. 30, the carrier said in its earnings filing Monday.

"Importantly, we believe we have the liquidity and financing options to create a strong bridge to at least $1 EPS and positive free cash flow in 2028," CFO Ursula Hurley said in a statement. "We remain focused on disciplined cost execution and capital management as we work toward sustained profitability and positive free cash flow."

Here is what JetBlue reported in the third quarter compared with Wall Street estimates compiled by LSEG:

JetBlue last posted an annual profit in 2019. The airline is in the middle of a multiyear turnaround plan that includes cutting cuts and boosting revenue by investing in premium seats, like a new domestic first class section that recently went on sale.

Fuel costs have eaten into airline profits across the board. JetBlue said travel demand has remained strong despite higher fares.

JetBlue's average fares rose more than 19% to $245.11 in the third quarter from $205.67 in the year-earlier period.

The airline has struggled not just with higher costs but also with competition from larger rivals.

It sought to acquire now-defunct carrier Spirit Airlines, but that deal was upended by a federal judge who ruled it was anticompetitive. A judge also blocked a regional partnership between JetBlue and American Airlines, which the airlines argued would help them compete better against carriers in the congested Northeast U.S. JetBlue now has a partnership with United Airlines.

The carrier expects to grow capacity no more than 4% in the current quarter. One bright spot, executives said, has been expanding at Fort Lauderdale-Hollywood International Airport, the former home hub of Spirit, which collapsed in May.

#545. UPS beats quarterly expectations, raises full-year guidance again

United Parcel Service on Tuesday reported third-quarter results that beat Wall Street expectations and raised its full-year guidance.

The guidance raise comes after the company already upped its outlook last quarter. UPS now said it's projecting full-year consolidated revenue of approximately $92.6 billion, up from its previous expectations of $91.2 billion, as well as fourth-quarter revenue of $25.7 billion.

Shares of UPS rose slightly in morning trading.

Here's how the company performed in its third quarter, compared with what Wall Street was expecting, based on a survey of analysts by LSEG:

For the quarter ended Sept. 30, UPS reported net income of $1.16 billion, or $1.37 per share, down slightly from net income of $1.31 billion, or $1.55, the year prior. Adjusting for one-time expenses, UPS reported earnings per share of $1.69. Revenue rose 6.7% to $22.9 billion.

In its domestic segment, the company said it saw a 4.5% increase in revenue, while its international segment had a 7.1% rise in revenue. Its supply chain solutions business saw an 18.2% revenue increase, which UPS said was primarily driven to growth in forwarding and logistics, including healthcare. On a call with analysts, CEO Carol Tomé said healthcare revenue exceeded $3 billion for the third consecutive quarter.

#525. GM will switch to Nasdaq from the NYSE on Nov. 17

DETROIT — General Motors plans to transfer shares of its common stock from the New York Stock Exchange to the Nasdaq as the automaker attempts to position itself more as a technology-focused company.

The Detroit automaker said Tuesday that it expects to begin trading as a Nasdaq-listed company on Nov. 17. Its ticker symbol is expected to remain "GM," the company said.

"GM's customer-focused, technology-driven strategy is creating value for our stockholders, making Nasdaq a natural home for our listing as we grow," GM CFO Paul Jacobson said in a release. "We're grateful for our long-standing partnership with the New York Stock Exchange and its remarkable legacy."

GM noted the move will provide eligibility for potential inclusion in the Nasdaq-100 Index.

Current shareholders do not need to take any action with respect to the transfer, the company said.

The switch marks a notable loss for the NYSE, as GM first listed on the exchange in 1911. The Detroit automaker also relisted on the stock exchange in 2010 following a government-backed bankruptcy.

GM follows other companies such as Walmart, DoorDash and Palantir Technologies that have transferred from the more traditional NYSE to the historically technology focused Nasdaq in recent years. Hilton Worldwide Holdings last week also announced plans to move trading from the NYSE to Nasdaq.

The key drivers for a listing transfer differ from company to company, but the Nasdaq in 2024 said common reasons include "index inclusion opportunities, cost effective fee structure, brand visibility partnership, access to Nasdaq's event space, and Nasdaq's suite of investor relations and ESG products."

GM's decision comes a week after the automaker held a technology-focused event where executives laid out the company's roadmap for artificial intelligence and self-driving vehicle technologies.

Shares of GM, which has been aggressively buying back stock, have more than doubled since 2024. GM stock closed Tuesday at $80.54 a share, up 5%. The company's market cap is roughly $64.4 billion.

#503. Coca-Cola hikes earnings outlook, even as it sees lackluster demand in North America

Coca-Cola on Tuesday raised its full-year outlook for the third straight quarter, despite lackluster demand in its home market.

For the full year, Coke now expects comparable earnings per share to grow at least 11%, up from its prior forecast of an increase in the range of 9% to 10%. The beverage giant also anticipates that its organic revenue will climb at least 5%, a slight increase from its previous outlook of approximately 5% growth.

CEO Henrique Braun attributed the company's strong results to its "speed and discipline" despite the "uneven environment," he said on the company's earnings conference call.

Looking ahead to 2027, the company expects consumer behavior to remain unchanged, CFO John Murphy said. In recent quarters, Braun and other executives have said North American shoppers are under pressure, stretched thin by inflation and economic uncertainty, and looking for value. But the beverage giant has also said consumers are still buying its drinks, from premium brands like Fairlife to its namesake soda.

Shares of the company fell less than 1% in morning trading on its gloomy forecast for the consumer environment.

Here's what the company reported compared with what Wall Street analysts surveyed by LSEG were expecting:

Coke reported fiscal third-quarter net income attributable to shareholders of $4.01 billion, or 93 cents per share, up from $3.7 billion, or 86 cents per share, a year earlier.

Excluding transaction gains and other items, Coke earned 91 cents per share.

Net sales rose 7% to $13.4 billion. Coke's organic revenue, which strips out acquisitions, divestitures and foreign currency, also climbed 7% in the quarter.

For the second straight quarter, the company's unit case volume increased 5%, helped by higher demand outside of North America.

Coke's home market reported flat volume. However, the company's North American business would have reported volume growth if not for the timing of the Fourth of July holiday and the monthslong shortage of Topo Chico, a sparkling water brand Coke owns, Murphy told CNBC.

"The underlying momentum remains quite strong," he said. "Consumer sentiment is largely unchanged in the sense that, wherever you are on the income scale, you're looking for value, and I think our system here has done a good job interpreting that."

In North America, Coke's at-home business is faring better than its away-from-home segment, which includes restaurants, movie theaters and amusement parks. The restaurant industry is still under pressure, and Coke expects that to continue through the rest of the year, Murphy told analysts on the company's earnings conference call.

Globally, Coke's sparkling soft drinks business saw volume increase 5% during the quarter, lifted by growth of Coke, Coke Zero Sugar and Diet Coke.

Diet Coke is having a "resurgence," Murphy told CNBC. Gen Z consumers in the U.S. have nicknamed it a "fridge cigarette," while consumers in India threw Diet Coke parties as shortages tied to the U.S.-Israeli war with Iran have made the drink scarce.

"It's a brand that's almost reinventing itself with the next generation of consumers," he said.

Coke has also gained market share as rival PepsiCo sees its soda business struggle, according to Murphy. Still, some of Coke's soda brands, like Sprite, have "some work to do."

Coke's water, sports, coffee and tea division also saw volume climb by 5%. Increased demand for its water, which includes its premium Smartwater brand, fueled the growth. Demand for its tea and coffee was flat, while sports drinks declined.

The company's juice, value-added dairy and plant-based beverages business reported flat volume, despite growth in brands like Fairlife and Minute Maid.
