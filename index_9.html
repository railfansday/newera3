#470. Novo says Wegovy injection lowers BMI and obesity rates in children in trial

Novo on Monday released full results from a late-stage trial showing its blockbuster Wegovy injection helped children ages 6 to under 12 with obesity shed weight, significantly lowering their body mass index.

Wegovy reduced BMI by an estimated 19.7 percentage points on average compared with a placebo at 68 weeks, with BMI falling 17.4% among children receiving the drug and rising 2.2% among those receiving a placebo. Those results assumed patients stayed on treatment during the trial.

When evaluating all children in the study, including those who discontinued treatment, Wegovy lowered BMI by an estimated average of 16.2 percentage points more than the placebo at 68 weeks. All 167 kids in the study received lifestyle modifications throughout the trial, including a ​reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity.

The detailed results mark an encouraging step forward in treating childhood obesity — a chronic disease that can carry serious cardiovascular, metabolic and psychosocial consequences but has had few treatment options for younger children. When left unaddressed, childhood obesity can extend into adulthood, bringing earlier and more severe health complications.

"This is a very significant disease burden in this very young patient population," said Dr. Andrea Traina, Novo's medical director lead of obesity.

In 2025, the disease affected an estimated roughly 177 million children and teens ages 5 to 19 worldwide, according to the World Obesity Federation. That figure is projected to rise to 228 million by 2040.

While Wegovy is already approved to treat obesity in children ages 12 and older, there are currently no approved medications for those under 12. The latest results offer evidence that Wegovy can potentially fill that treatment gap, safely and effectively reducing the severity of the disease in children as young as 6.

In Novo's trial, 85% of children started with moderate to severe obesity, which corresponds to BMI thresholds of 35 and above and 40 or higher, respectively. Nearly half already had at least one obesity-related complication, such as high blood pressure, high cholesterol or sleep apnea.

But more than 40% of children treated with Wegovy were no longer classified as having obesity after 68 weeks of the trial, assuming patients stayed on treatment. Novo initially shared that result in September.

"We are driving towards control of this chronic disease in that patient population when they are still so young and at such high risk for more advanced and accelerated disease progression because they developed it so early," Traina said.

Novo said Wegovy's overall safety and tolerability among children — how well patients managed the drug — were consistent with those seen in ​trials ⁠with adults. The company added that it observed no safety concerns related to growth or pubertal development in the kids.

Still, the two-year study does not establish whether treating obesity earlier can change its long-term trajectory, or what treatment and maintenance might look like as children grow into adulthood, Traina said.

"That is something that is still an evidence gap," she said, pointing to the potential of future research.

#466. Why a Crusoe investor is betting on solar and batteries to power future AI data centers

The problem of supplying power to AI data centers has become one of the central debates of the AI era. In just a few years, the U.S. grid has gone from basically zero growth to adding several gigawatts a year.

“Demand is growing faster than people appreciate,” Brook Porter, partner at G2 Venture Partners (G2VP), told TechCrunch. “It’s atoms, not bits, and the world is in the process of recognizing the realities of those challenges.”

It’s been a bumpy road. Because it takes a while to scale supply chains, companies have been securing power in any form they can. In many cases, that has meant buying, renting, and deploying natural gas power plants, but Porter thinks that hyperscalers and data center developers should look at the bigger picture if they want to move faster.

That picture involves solar paired with energy storage, based on an analysis Porter and his colleagues at G2VP recently conducted on the state of the data center power market.

“Global capacity of natural gas is only about 60 or 70 gigawatts per year, where solar is already at well over a terawatt,” he said. “Probably the most important lesson that we’ve learned over the last 20 years of backing companies is that Wright’s Law is the thing you want to leverage — making the same thing over and over again.”

Both solar and most forms of long-duration energy storage are built on small, modular technologies that can be manufactured at massive scale. “This is about speed and scale and economics, and we can win across all those dimensions,” Porter said.

G2VP had an early window into AI data center development, having led Crusoe’s Series C. “We’ve had a front-row seat at what’s been happening at that company, how they’ve evolved, how the different waves of data centers have evolved,” Porter said.

The venture firm estimates that data center investment will top $4 trillion until the end of the decade. As the next wave takes shape, the firm sees two different paths for data centers: small, on-grid facilities with their own generating capacity, and larger data centers powered by solar and long-duration energy storage.

“The big idea is to go off-grid,” Porter said. “What that would enable you to do is build with renewables in a way that’s independent of the grid. You can determine your own fate, go around some of the constraints of permitting, whether that’s access to the grid or air permits. You can accelerate your time to market dramatically.”

In recent years, energy storage has been synonymous with lithium-ion batteries, which are too expensive to compete with fossil fuels for round-the-clock power. But a new generation of long-duration energy storage products from the likes of Form Energy and G2VP portfolio company Antora Energy have brought the cost per megawatt-hour down significantly.

Even though the technologies might be a few years away from broad acceptance among data center developers, Porter said they should start thinking about them now.

“It’s analogous to surfing the biggest waves off of Mavericks here in Northern California. The wave is so big that you really have to be paddling and moving and be incredibly nimble to catch it. If you’re at a standstill, it’s just gonna blow you by,” he said.

#435. UK warns AI-driven cyberattacks are exposing years of weak security

The U.K. government’s cybersecurity unit says it has seen a “marked rise” in AI-driven cyberattacks in recent months, as hackers increasingly rely on autonomous hacking tools to identify and exploit simple weaknesses to break into systems and networks.

The U.K. National Cybersecurity Center (NCSC) said it has seen several cyber incidents since September involving autonomous hacking tools, such as penetration testing tools used by defenders to stress-test their cybersecurity defenses. These incidents have caused intrusions at organizations and resulted in downtime, the unit said in a blog post.

The NCSC says that while none of the vulnerabilities being exploited are new, the speed at which these AI tools are able to carry out simple hacks has changed.

The rise in AI-related cyberattacks coincides with the broader release earlier this year of open source autonomous penetration testing tools like Artex and Cairn, among others, which anyone can download and use at no cost. The U.S. Federal Bureau of Investigation recently warned that malicious hackers are using AI penetration testing tools to carry out cyberattacks at unprecedented speeds.

While cyberattacks and intrusions are on the rise, the message from London is that many of these attacks are preventable with basic cybersecurity defenses and best practices.

“Notably, the issues being exploited currently are not novel memory corruption vulnerabilities (or even known but unpatched vulnerabilities), but unmitigated weaknesses that are often the result of years of technical debt that organisations have accumulated,” the NCSC wrote.

The unit said that agentic hacking tools often look for exposed or internet-facing systems, then quickly run through lists of weak or easy-to-guess passwords or abuse API endpoints often used by developers to access data.

The NCSC said that AI “does not make established cyber security controls obsolete,” but rather that “building strong foundations has never mattered more.”

The unit added that AI has made it easier to find vulnerabilities and faster to exploit them, warning that weaknesses that have gone unnoticed for years can now be found in minutes.

Like other AI apps on the market, Maple allows users to engage in a chat experience where they ask questions and get answers. The models themselves run inside a trusted execution environment (TEE) — meaning a server with hardware encryption on it — within a U.S.-based data center. (Maple also offers support for European customers through a partner that runs its server in Europe.)

This allows Maple to protect its users’ data, even from outsiders — like if hackers breached its system or the government came with a subpoena, for instance. All it could do would be to hand over an encrypted file.

“We just simply don’t want access to it,” Suman says of customers’ data. “That’s not part of our business model. And we also don’t want it to be part of our threat model.”

#412. Even the best AI agents struggle with everyday life

AI companies have spent the last couple of months marketing personal AI agents as tools to take over the digital drudgery of human existence — and millions have been sold on the vision, handing over their shopping schedules, travel plans, and everyday responsibilities to AI assistants. But new research suggests that even the most advanced AI models struggle to reliably and safely navigate the messy details of people’s lives.

According to ShellBench, a new benchmark for everyday agent work, even the most advanced models from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Meta failed more than a third of the time. Across all models tested, 15.7% of attempts failed at least one safety requirement, often leading to unintended consequences.

ShellBench was developed in collaboration with researchers at The OpenClaw Foundation, Parsewave, Hugging Face, Red Hat, and UC Berkeley, drawing on more than 10 million anonymized, real-world AI agent interactions to see how agents perform on the everyday work people actually delegate to them.

The research comes as personal AI assistants are finding new popularity among developers, with Meta, OpenAI, and Anthropic all fighting for a piece of the market with Muse, Dots, and Claude Duet, respectively.

In October, Meta said more than 10 million people in the U.S. and Canada had set up its personal AI agent, Muse, within six weeks of launch, with about a quarter using it daily. That number is expected to grow as Meta implants Muse directly into Instagram DMs. Despite growing popularity among consumers, we still have little evidence about how reliable AI personal assistant products actually are.

ShellBench suggests that the unreliable nature of most everyday data sources still causes problems for many state-of-the-art systems. A successful agent should be able to navigate messy personal workspaces containing emails, calendar entries, spreadsheets, receipts, chat logs, outdated information, and phishing attempts, and figure out which information is relevant, what is private, and how to deliver the requested outcome without creating new problems.

It seems many of today’s AI agents are still not up to the task. Not only did even the highest-performing agents frequently fail to meet the requirements of a task, but they also lacked consistency. When researchers ran each task three times under identical conditions, OpenAI’s GPT-6 Astra, the highest-scoring model, nailed all three attempts less than half the time.

For autonomous assistants that are meant to run in the background without supervision, it’s a problem if users need to run an agent multiple times to get a result they trust.

Additionally, about 10% of otherwise correct attempts failed at least one safety criterion. Across the models and harnesses tested, agents failed to, for example, adequately account for a child’s school pickup 79.5% of the time. When handling laptop custody records, agents failed a requirement to identify and withhold a sensitive bank upload token in 18.6% of attempts. In another task, agents recommended duplicate or incorrect invoice payments 40.3% of the time.

The findings highlight a major issue for users adopting consumer personal AI assistants, namely that there’s always a risk that an agent can create more headaches in the course of trying to fulfill an otherwise ordinary request.

While AI benchmarks have largely focused on coding, professional work, or testing on specific tasks, ShellBench is attempting to evaluate how models perform on multi-step personal tasks within different agent harnesses — the software that controls how a model accesses information, uses tools, and takes actions.

While the researchers found model choice had a greater overall impact on performance than harness choice, among the more capable models, AI agent performance became more dependent on the harness.

For example, OpenAI’s GPT-6 Astra outperformed Anthropic’s Claude Fable 5.2 when operating within the Pi harness, but it performed worse when both models were tested within Claude Code.

Meta’s Muse Wonder Full scored 56% in Pi, but only 38.1% in Claude Code. Meta’s Muse Wonder 1.0, meanwhile, averaged a success rate of 39.7% across the five harnesses tested.

Notably, while the researchers tested models from Meta, OpenAI, and Anthropic, they didn’t evaluate them within the native harnesses of Muse, Dots, or Claude Duet, so the results may not be representative of the experience millions of consumers are having with the product today.

#308. U.S. officially loses measles elimination status amid ongoing outbreaks

The U.S. on Tuesday lost its official designation as a measles-free nation, more than 25 years after it eliminated the highly contagious virus.

The Pan American Health Organization announced the decision, which came after an independent panel of eight scientific experts convened last week to consider the country's epidemiological evidence. The director of PAHO, the regional branch of the World Health Organization, made the final call that measles is endemic — or always present — in the U.S. following 12 months of continuous transmission.

In other words, measles is spreading regularly in the country again, with ongoing outbreaks sparking new ones.

"The commission concluded that endemic measles transmission has now been reestablished in Bolivia, Mexico and the United States, and recommended that their measles elimination status be withdrawn — a recommendation which I have accepted," Dr. Jarbas Barbosa, PAHO director, said during a news briefing on Tuesday.

It's a devastating step backward for American public health, as declining childhood vaccination rates and vaccine hesitancy fuel outbreaks across the country that have sickened thousands of people and led to a tragic string of preventable deaths. Among the deaths where vaccination status was reported, all were unvaccinated.

More than 5,400 measles cases had been reported nationwide in 2026 as of mid-November, marking the country's worst outbreak in more than three decades. States have confirmed 10 deaths associated with the disease so far in 2026: seven in Pennsylvania, one in New York and two in Wisconsin.

The commission found that out of 52 U.S. states and territories it tracked, 48 experienced measles outbreaks during the reporting period between January 2025 and January 2026, PAHO Assistant Director Dr. Rhonda Sealey-Thomas said during the briefing. But Barbosa said the countries are already taking action to control outbreaks, such as strengthening vaccination, surveillance for timely case detection and outbreak response efforts.

The U.S. in particular has ramped up vaccination efforts to close immunity gaps, particularly in areas with low immunization coverage experiencing outbreaks, said Dr. Daniel Salas, executive manager of PAHO's Special Program for Comprehensive Immunization. He said PAHO expects the U.S. to present its corrective action plan in the coming weeks, outlining strategies to regain its designation.

In a Tuesday release, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention said it will continue to ensure all communities have the resources to prevent and respond to outbreaks and is committed to working with states and other local partners to restore the U.S.'s measles elimination status.

CDC Director Dr. Erica Schwartz said in the release that the measles elimination designation "is not what protects Americans. Vaccination does." She said the agency has invested $100 million in state and local health departments to reach communities where transmission is occurring.

Last week, Schwartz told Reuters that the agency is going to be "doing the same exact thing that we've been doing today" if the U.S. loses its measles-free status.

The decision also poses a new test for the Trump administration and its Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., who has taken steps to change recommendations for the childhood measles, mumps and rubella, or MMR, vaccine and has a long history of questioning its safety without evidence.

But Kennedy, in a Tuesday X post, said he continues to recommend MMR vaccination as "the most effective way to prevent measles."

"We are working to close vaccination gaps and rebuild public trust," Kennedy said. "We will answer families' questions honestly about vaccine benefits and risks and work with trusted health care providers and community leaders to prevent measles, limit its spread, and support timely treatment."

CNBC previously reported on how the return of measles in the U.S. exposes weaknesses in the nation's public health system that raise concerns about the return of other eliminated diseases. The resurgence of measles exposes eroding trust in health institutions and growing challenges in persuading people to protect themselves, their children and their communities.

Public health experts previously told CNBC that the return of measles ultimately boils down to fewer children getting vaccinated.

The MMR vaccine is about 97% effective against measles after two doses, while a single dose is 93% effective, according to the CDC. Yet data aggregated by the CDC shows that vaccination coverage for that shot among U.S. kindergarteners fell to 92.4% in the 2025-2026 school year, which is below the 95% threshold public health officials generally recommend to prevent sustained transmission.

The American Medical Association urged the public to get vaccinated after PAHO's announcement.

"We must treat the current measles outbreaks as an urgent call to rebuild trust in science-based recommendations and restore strong, fully-funded public health infrastructure to protect families and communities and prevent the return of diseases once eliminated," AMA President Dr. Willie Underwood said in a statement.

In a separate statement, the pharmaceutical industry's largest lobbying group called on policymakers to provide "clear, accurate information about the seriousness of measles infection and the effectiveness of vaccines against it, restore and sustain high vaccination coverage in every community, strengthen vaccination efforts, and invest in disease surveillance and rapid outbreak response."

"We have the tools to eliminate measles again and what we need now is the commitment to use them," said Dr. Michael Ybarra, chief medical officer of the Pharmaceutical Research and Manufacturers of America.

The U.S. now joins Canada, Spain and the U.K., which all recently lost their measles-free status after a review by experts at the World Health Organization. While the U.S. dropped out of the WHO, it remains a member of PAHO, its Americas branch.
