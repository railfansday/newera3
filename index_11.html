#68. Anthropic IPO raises over $81 billion in largest offering on record, topping SpaceX

Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 per share on Wednesday, above the expected range, setting the stage for the Claude creator's eagerly awaited Nasdaq listing and a historic moment for the artificial intelligence industry.

The company, along with its seven co-founders, upsized the offering, selling about 980 million shares, according to a press release. That brings the amount raised to $81.3 billion, surpassing SpaceX, which held a record IPO in June. Elon Musk's company raised $75 billion initially and a total of $85.7 billion after underwriters exercised their overallotment.

Anthropic's fully diluted market cap sits at around $1.5 trillion. The stock is expected to begin trading on the Nasdaq on Thursday under the ticker symbol "ANT."

The listing is a major test for the AI industry, which has experienced a set of recent shocks, including a dramatic backlash that was felt at the polls in the midterm elections and last week's ouster of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang. The market was already jittery on concerns that rising interest rates will make debt unaffordable for many data center developers and neoclouds.

After publicly unveiling its prospectus in October, Anthropic updated the document last week with an expected price range between $70 and $78 per share.  The company had been planning to sell 825 million shares.

Tech's biggest companies have significant exposure to Anthropic, as an investor, partner or customer. Amazon and Google both own stakes valued at over $100 billion, and a group of tech companies, including Google, Microsoft, Nvidia and Advanced Micro Devices agreed to collectively invest $26 billion at the IPO price. Anthropic, meanwhile, is a buyer of costly systems from Nvidia, Google, Amazon and AMD.

"I think if we do this right, we can create an age of miracles where humanity is able to solve problems that have eluded us for millennia," Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei said in Anthropic's roadshow video. "We have to make sure that all the risks, all the ways that things can go wrong with this technology, we very carefully address those risks as we bring the benefits and as we have a viable business at the same time."

Amodei co-founded Anthropic in 2021 alongside a handful of other researchers less than six years ago, after the group collectively defected from OpenAI. The company is best known for developing a family of AI models called Claude, and it primarily sells its products and services to other businesses.

According to the latest prospectus, each co-founder is selling 3.38 million shares for total proceeds of $1.96 billion.

Anthropic has been racing to maintain an edge over OpenAI and Google in the enterprise market, and it experienced a period of explosive growth in 2026, driven largely by the popularity of its AI coding tool, Claude Code. OpenAI has confidentially filed for an IPO and has said a debut is likely in 2027.

For the nine months ended Sept. 30, Anthropic said, it generated $33.24 billion in revenue, up roughly 1,200% from the same period last year, according to its prospectus. The company reported a $104 billion net loss during the nine-month period, though most of that was due to the change in fair value of convertible notes and derivative liabilities. Anthropic lost $25.1 billion over the same stretch in 2025, the filing said.

Anthropic said in an amended filing that its run-rate revenue was roughly $81 billion at the end of October, up $10 billion from the previous month.

The company has faced plenty of challenges during its meteoric rise over its positions on safety, regulation and national security, leading to two high-profile disputes with the U.S. government this year. Its prospectus included more than 90 pages of risk factors for investors to consider ahead of its IPO.

"Our aim is to help humankind chart a safe course through this technological evolution, open new frontiers of discovery, and bring forth an age of miracles," Anthropic's co-founders wrote in the company's prospectus. "Everyone who owns our stock, whether an institution or an individual, becomes part of that effort."
