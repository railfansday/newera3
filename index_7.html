#780. JPMorgan Chase says temporary software bug is affecting consumer banking

JPMorgan Chase reported a "technical issue" that was affecting some consumer banking services Monday.

The bank said a "temporary issue related to a software bug in on-premise hardware systems" was causing some customer transactions to take longer than usual, though it said customers can still access their accounts and funds.

"There is no evidence it is cyber-related," a JPMorgan spokesperson said in a statement to CNBC.

"This is not a systemic issue — other banks may have been impacted by slowness in transactions only to the extent that they were a party to a transaction with us," the spokesperson said. "We're working diligently to resolve this by the end of the day today."

CNBC's Eamon Javers reported there was no indication of bad actors involved.

Still, other major banks were listed in outage reports as transactions between Chase and other institutions appeared to get held up. Users reported errors as early as 11:30 a.m. ET.

A representative for U.S. Bank said that company was "not directly impacted. However, clients attempting to send or receive Zelle payments with another financial institution may experience transaction delays."

Zelle is the peer-to-peer payments system backed by many of the largest U.S. banks, including JPMorgan.

The outage reports come days after separate, widespread issues with Microsoft Outlook and communication systems used by air traffic control centers.

— CNBC's Leslie Picker contributed to this report.

#750. United Airlines resolves system outage that affected departing flights

United Airlines on Monday said it had resolved a "widespread system outage" that briefly affected departing flights.

"We have resolved a technology issue that delayed flight departures," the airline said in a statement. "While some flights were delayed, there was no impact on flights in progress, or on United Express flights.

A United spokesperson said there was no indication that the outage was a cyberattack.

The Air Line Pilots Association, which represents pilots for United and other carriers, said in an earlier message to members seen by CNBC that the operational outage was "affecting multiple pilot facing applications" and that United could not "provide outbound flights with final weights."

United's outage came three days after a nationwide ground stop halted departures across the country for more than four hours, snarling travel and leading to widespread delays and cancellations over the weekend.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy blamed the issues that led to Friday's ground stop on a router in Arkansas that overwhelmed systems that feed radar and other data for air traffic control. Federal Aviation Administration Administrator Bryan Bedford said the upgrades were part of "routine" maintenance to switch over from copper wires to fiber optic.

There were significantly fewer flight delays and cancellations Monday than had been reported Sunday, according to FlightAware data.

There was no indication that United's outage Monday was related to the issues that caused Friday's ground stop.

— CNBC's Leslie Josephs and Phil LeBeau contributed to this report.

#723. Disney beats fourth-quarter estimates, targets $3 billion in savings through cost-cutting efforts

Disney on Tuesday reported quarterly earnings and revenue that exceeded analysts' expectations and announced it's targeting $3 billion in cost savings by fiscal 2029.

Those cost-cutting efforts include several rounds of layoffs announced in recent months, Disney's chief financial officer, Hugh Johnston, told CNBC. The most recent round, which seeks to reduce costs by up to 20%, was reported earlier this month.

Future cost-saving measures will include reductions in administrative and other general expenses, Johnston said, while adding, "We do expect more layoffs."

Disney CEO Josh D'Amaro told investors on the company's earnings call that the job cuts are "not easy."

"They affect people who care deeply about Disney and have contributed so much to this company," he said. "At the same time, these steps are important if we want to be a company that moves faster, adapts to a rapidly changing environment, and keeps investing in the content and technology that will drive our growth."

The cuts will help Disney fund its $26 billion in projected content spend for fiscal 2027, a 9% increase from fiscal 2026.

The entertainment giant is also forecasting double-digit growth in earnings per share in the next fiscal year, as well as segment operating income growth of around 8%.

Disney noted that the majority of that growth will be concentrated in the first half of the year due to the theatrical debut of "Avengers: Doomsday" and a boost from the Super Bowl, which will air on Disney's ESPN and ABC networks.

"The growth is really going to come in the first half of this year," Johnston said. "That's the opposite of what we saw in '26, where the growth was backloaded."

The company will also increase its annual dividend to $2 a share, a 33% increase from the previously declared dividend. It will be payable in two installments of $1 per share, one in January and one in July.

Here's how Disney performed in its fiscal fourth quarter ended Oct. 3, compared with what Wall Street expected, according to LSEG:

Disney reported net income for the fiscal fourth quarter of $2.61 billion, or $1.51 per share, compared with $1.31 billion, or 73 cents per share, during the same period the year prior.

Adjusting for one-time items, including severance and other restructuring charges as well as the value of intangible assets, Disney reported earnings per share of $1.77.

The company's revenue for the quarter was 16% higher year over year, and results were once again lifted by its parks and streaming divisions.

Disney's experiences segment, which includes its theme parks and cruises, reported nearly $10.11 billion in revenue, up 15% year over year. Operating income for the unit increased 20% to $2.25 billion.

The division is already a top performer for the company, but it benefited in the fiscal fourth quarter because of an extra calendar week compared with the previous fiscal year.

While international attendance continues to lag at Disney's domestic parks, it is being offset by domestic attendance, especially at the Walt Disney World resort in Orlando, Florida, Johnston said.

Growth at Disneyland Paris was driven by recent investments including an expansion area called World of Frozen. Attendance at the Hong Kong and Shanghai parks remained soft because of broad economic challenges in those regions, according to Disney.

Still, D'Amaro said he believes the growth in parks will be sustainable through next year, especially with new additions, including the "Monsters, Inc."-themed land "Monstropolis" opening at Walt Disney World in 2027.

"Q1 forward resort bookings at Walt Disney World are pacing up compared to the prior year," he said. "And new capacity and new experiences will start coming online in 2027. We keep investing to make the experience fresh, and what we see is that drives repeat visits."

The company expects operating income for the experiences segment to grow in the high-single digits in fiscal 2027. Growth is expected to be front-loaded as the company laps the launch of the Disney Adventure cruise ship, which set sail in March.

Over at its entertainment division, Disney saw revenue increase 17% to $11.89 billion. Operating income nearly doubled to $1.33 billion.

Revenue from streaming, specifically, rose 19% to $6.1 billion, Disney said.

The segment benefited from the extra week on the fiscal calendar as well as from growth in subscription and affiliate fees. Additionally, the company saw higher theatrical distribution revenue from titles like "Toy Story 5."

Disney said Tuesday it expects operating income for the entertainment division to grow in the high-single digits in the coming fiscal year.

The company's sports division saw revenue increase 16% in the fiscal fourth quarter, reaching $4.61 billion. Operating income for the segment surged more than 50% to $1.39 billion.

Subscription and affiliate fees fueled this growth alongside an increase in advertising revenue.

The company expects its sports division's operating income to grow by double digits in fiscal 2027. It noted that the fiscal first quarter of the year will see some declines because of the timing of College Football Playoff games and the New Year's window. However, the fiscal second quarter will benefit from the college championship as well as the broadcast of Super Bowl 61.

Disney's outlook for overall and segment results for fiscal 2027 excludes the impact of a 53rd week in fiscal 2026.

D'Amaro has made advancing technology a pillar of the company's growth strategy. In September, Disney hired Karandeep Anand in the newly created position of chief technology officer.

"Technology is central to how we connect fans to more of Disney and drive returns on our investment," D'Amaro said on the earnings call. "[Anand has] been energized by how much opportunity he sees to move faster and go further in product and technology. Done right, that means serving our fans even better, working more efficiently and being more innovative across every one of our businesses."

Disney announced a partnership with Adobe earlier this month to develop generative artificial intelligence models.

D'Amaro told investors that the team is exploring ways to use AI to make booking trips to Disney parks easier.

"There's actually a vacation planning tool that's in beta right now at Walt Disney World. We built that with our partners at Google, and I've had a chance to experiment with it myself," he said. "It'll be incredibly helpful for our guests who are booking trips. With these AI features, it can feel like you've got someone sitting right by your side, like a personal concierge, that plans and responds to the way that you want to actually book your trip to Walt Disney World."

The entertainment company has also expanded its short-form content, adding a vertical video channel within the Disney+ mobile app. The company had previously announced a partnership with TikTok to bring creator content to the streaming app.

Here's how Home Depot performed in its fiscal third quarter compared with what Wall Street was expecting, based on a survey of analysts by LSEG:
